Dogs, Wildlife & the Law

Scope: This guide summarises selected rules relevant to England. Local restrictions and protected-site rules may also apply; check the linked official sources for your location.

A practical guide to controlling dogs responsibly, protecting wildlife and checking the rules before walks.

Back to Animal Articles
Animal Information Hub UK

Keep dogs under control

Choose a lead or long line when you cannot reliably prevent your dog from chasing, approaching wildlife, livestock or other visitors. Follow signs and local restrictions, and put your dog on a lead where an order requires it. A dog being friendly does not mean every person or animal wants to be approached.

In England, councils can use Public Spaces Protection Orders (PSPOs) to restrict dogs in particular places, require leads or set other conditions. Check the council website for the park, beach, nature reserve or open space you plan to visit. Rules can differ between nearby locations.

Protect nesting birds and wildlife

Wild birds, their eggs and active nests receive legal protection. It can be an offence to intentionally kill, injure or take a wild bird, or to take, damage or destroy a nest while it is in use or being built. Schedule 1 birds have additional protection against intentional or reckless disturbance at the nest and of dependent young.

Keep your dog away from nests, reedbeds, ground-nesting areas and wildlife refuges. Do not let a dog chase deer, birds or other wildlife. On sensitive sites, follow the land manager’s instructions and any seasonal restrictions. If you find an injured wild animal, keep your dog back and contact an appropriate wildlife rescue or veterinary service rather than attempting risky handling.

Practical habits on walks

  • Read signs at entrances and follow local lead requirements.
  • Use a lead near livestock, wildlife or narrow paths where you cannot create space.
  • Prevent chasing, digging or entering fenced conservation areas.
  • Pick up dog waste and dispose of it properly.
  • Recall training helps, but do not rely on it where wildlife or livestock could be harmed.

Where the rules apply

This page focuses on England and is not a complete statement of wildlife or dog-control law across the UK. Different legislation, site designations and local orders may apply. If you are unsure whether an activity is permitted, contact the relevant council or land manager before acting.
