Lost & Found Animals

Practical first steps and useful contacts if an animal has gone missing or you have found an animal that may need help.

Lost a dog?
PHOTO SPACE · Help reunite pets
Important

If an animal is injured, in immediate danger or needs urgent veterinary attention, contact a veterinary practice or emergency service promptly. Do not put yourself or the animal at risk trying to catch or handle a frightened animal. The phone numbers below were supplied for this resource and have not been independently verified here; check they are current before relying on them.

Dogs

What to do if your dog is missing

  1. Check the microchip record. Contact your microchip database to confirm your contact details are correct and report your dog missing. If you do not know which database holds the record, your vet may be able to help.
  2. Contact your local council’s dog warden. Report the missing dog and ask about local procedures for dogs found straying.
  3. Register the dog with DogLost. Create a report and follow its current instructions for sharing details with local helpers.
  4. Share public posts. Use clear, recent photos, the area and time last seen, distinguishing features and a safe contact method. Avoid sharing information that could put you at risk.
  5. Use local lost-and-found groups. Share one public post where possible, then share that post into relevant groups so updates remain in one place. Keep a note of where you have reported the loss and be cautious of scams or requests for money to release your pet.

Cats

Lost, found and feral cats

Five important things to remember

  • Cats with established routines can sometimes disappear for a while, but report a missing cat and begin searching if you are concerned.
  • Feral cats are domestic cats that are not socialised to people. They may be very frightened and should be approached cautiously.
  • Keep your cat microchipped and ensure the registered contact details are current.
  • A cat outdoors is not necessarily stray. Check for an owner before assuming it needs rehoming.
  • If a cat appears ill or injured, seek advice from a vet or local animal welfare organisation.

If your cat is missing

Check your home, garden, sheds and other small hiding places carefully. Ask neighbours to check garages and outbuildings, and share a clear photo with local groups. Contact your microchip database, local vets and animal welfare organisations.

If you find a cat

A healthy, well-groomed cat may have a home nearby. Ask neighbours and share a photo with local groups. If safe to do so, a vet or animal welfare organisation may be able to scan for a microchip. Avoid feeding or moving the cat unnecessarily if doing so may interfere with identifying its owner or put the cat at risk.

Stray or feral cats

Be cautious with nervous, frightened, sick or injured cats. Do not attempt to grab or restrain a cat that may bite or scratch. Ask a vet or experienced local rescue for safe advice. Only use a carrier or humane trap if it is safe and appropriate, and seek experienced help where needed.

If you find a cat that appears dead

If there is any doubt that the cat has died, contact a vet immediately. If the cat appears deceased, avoid handling it with bare hands and contact a local vet, council or animal welfare organisation for advice about scanning for a microchip and the appropriate next steps.

If no owner can be found

Contact local animal welfare organisations for advice about lost-and-found registers and the correct process. Do not assume you can immediately rehome a cat; there may be an owner actively searching for them.
