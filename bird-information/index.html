Bird information

Species-aware guidance for companion birds, with attention to environment, social needs, enrichment, diet and health.

Browse animal categories
Practical information · Welfare first
Start here

Key areas to consider

Use these topics as a starting point, and adapt guidance to the species and individual animal.

01 · Guide

Species-specific needs

Birds vary widely. Research the needs of the particular species, including social contact, space, flight, temperature and daily activity.

02 · Guide

Diet and water

Use reputable, species-specific feeding guidance. Avoid assuming that one diet suits every bird and ask an avian veterinary professional about changes.

03 · Guide

Environment and enrichment

Provide safe space, appropriate perches, opportunities to forage and suitable activities. Check the home for fumes, hazards and escape risks.

04 · Guide

Health and handling

Birds can hide illness. Seek advice from an avian vet if you notice changes in appetite, droppings, breathing, posture or activity. Handle calmly and only as needed.

In-depth guide

Looking after companion birds

Birds differ greatly by species, so begin by identifying the exact species and researching its adult needs before choosing housing or diet.

Environment and daily activity

Provide safe, appropriately sized housing with room to move and suitable perches, bathing or foraging opportunities where relevant. Birds need daily activity and social interaction appropriate to their species. Avoid placing cages in kitchens or areas exposed to smoke, aerosols or overheated non-stick cookware fumes.

Diet and enrichment

Use a balanced, species-appropriate diet based on reputable avian guidance. Fresh water must always be available. Enrichment should encourage safe foraging, movement and choice; check toys for loose threads, sharp edges or parts that could be swallowed.

Health checks

Watch for changes in appetite, droppings, posture, breathing, vocalisation and activity. Birds may hide illness, so contact an avian veterinary practice promptly if something changes. Arrange routine care with a vet familiar with birds.
