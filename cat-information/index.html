Everyday essentials

Understanding your cat

Cats communicate through their whole body. Look at ear position, eyes, tail, posture, movement and context together rather than relying on one signal.

01 · Communication

Read body language

Watch your cat’s ears, eyes, tail, posture and vocalisations. Give them space if they seem worried, tense or overstimulated.

02 · Choice and trust

Respect their boundaries

Let your cat approach and choose whether to interact. Hiding, turning away, freezing, swishing the tail or flattening the ears can mean they need space.

03 · Health

Notice signs of pain

Cats can hide pain. Changes in facial expression, appetite, grooming, movement, toileting or social behaviour deserve attention and may need veterinary advice.

04 · Home life

Make the home cat-friendly

Provide hiding places, elevated resting spots, scratching surfaces, play, clean litter trays and access to food and water without competition from other cats.

Behaviour

Feline body language

Observing ear signals alongside the tail, posture, eyes and vocalisations can help you understand how a cat may be feeling. Consider the whole situation and allow the cat to move away.

Feline Grimace Scale

The Feline Grimace Scale is a structured tool for assessing facial expressions associated with acute pain in cats. It can support observation but cannot replace veterinary assessment.

Handling

How to pick up a cat

Not every cat enjoys being picked up. Approach calmly, allow them to choose whether to interact, support their body fully if lifting is necessary, and put them down if they show signs of distress. Never lift a cat by the scruff.

How to handle cats respectfully

Approach slowly and avoid cornering a cat. Offer a hand only if they seem comfortable, and let them move away. If a cat must be lifted, support the chest and hindquarters and keep them close to your body. Stop if they struggle, freeze, hiss, growl or try to bite. For a frightened or injured cat, ask a vet or qualified animal professional for safe handling advice.

Everyday care

Simple ways to support feline wellbeing

Food and water

Offer a complete, age-appropriate diet and fresh water. Monitor appetite, drinking and weight, and ask your vet before making major dietary changes.

Litter trays and resources

Keep litter trays clean, accessible and in quiet places. In multi-cat homes, provide multiple separated resources so cats do not have to compete.

Play and enrichment

Use safe toys and short play sessions that allow stalking and chasing. Rotate activities and provide scratching surfaces and places to climb or hide.

Routine veterinary care

Ask your vet about vaccinations, parasite prevention, dental health and neutering. Contact a veterinary practice for sudden or persistent changes in behaviour or health.

When to contact a vet

Seek veterinary advice if your cat has a sudden change in behaviour, appears painful, stops eating, struggles to breathe or has difficulty passing urine. Inability to pass urine is an emergency, particularly in male cats. If you are concerned, do not rely on online information alone.
