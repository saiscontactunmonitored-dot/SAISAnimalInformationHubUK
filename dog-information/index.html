Everyday essentials

Supporting your dog's wellbeing

Start with the basics, then explore the guides below for more specific questions.

01 · Health

Preventative care

Keep up with routine veterinary checks and discuss vaccination, parasite prevention, dental care and diet with your vet.

02 · Behaviour

Kind, reward-based training

Focus on communication, choice and reinforcement. Avoid approaches that rely on fear, pain or intimidation.

03 · Safety

Prepare for hazards

Know how to respond to possible poisoning, overheating, bites and sudden illness. Contact a vet promptly if you are worried.

04 · Everyday life

Food, equipment and travel

Choose products and activities for your individual dog's size, health, age and needs. Check fit and safety regularly.

Equipment and seasonal safety

Harness fitting, heatstroke and aquatic algae

Some topics need a little more context before you follow advice.

Equipment

Harness and collar fitting guide

Measure your dog's chest around the widest part of the rib cage, following the harness maker's instructions. Measure the neck where the chosen product is designed to sit, and check the manufacturer's sizing chart. Weight can be a secondary guide, but measurements and fit matter most. You should be able to check the fit comfortably and regularly.

Warm weather

Heatstroke in dogs

Heatstroke is an emergency. If you suspect it, contact a vet immediately and follow their instructions. Do not wait for symptoms to worsen. Read the guidance from Allwinds Dog First Aid and review the linked RVC heatstroke paper (Google Drive preview).

Water safety

Aquatic vegetation and algae

Green water, surface scum or mats of algae can be difficult to identify by sight. Some blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) can produce toxins that are dangerous to dogs. Keep dogs away from suspicious water and surface scum, prevent them drinking it or licking their coat after contact, and contact a vet urgently if exposure is possible. Do not rely on colour alone to identify whether water is safe.

When in doubt, contact your vet

Online guides cannot diagnose illness or determine whether a particular dog is safe after exposure to a hazard. For urgent symptoms, suspected poisoning, possible algae exposure or an adder bite, seek veterinary advice immediately.
