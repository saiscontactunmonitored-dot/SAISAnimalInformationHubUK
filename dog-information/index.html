Key areas to consider

Use these topics as a starting point, and adapt guidance to the animal and circumstances.

01 · Guide

Everyday care

Build a reliable routine around suitable food, fresh water, exercise, rest, grooming and regular veterinary care.

02 · Guide

Behaviour and training

Use reward-based, welfare-centred training. Give your dog time, choice and distance when they are worried or overwhelmed.

03 · Guide

Health and prevention

Keep vaccinations, parasite prevention and routine checks under veterinary guidance. Seek prompt advice for new or worsening symptoms.

04 · Guide

Home and enrichment

Provide a safe resting place, appropriate activities and opportunities to sniff, explore and interact at a pace that suits the individual dog.

In-depth guide

A practical routine for dog welfare

A good routine supports physical health, emotional security and the dog’s individual needs. Breed, age, health, previous experience and temperament all affect what is suitable.

Daily essentials

  • Provide fresh water, a balanced diet suitable for the dog’s life stage, a comfortable resting area and regular opportunities to toilet.
  • Plan exercise and enrichment around the individual dog. Sniffing, exploring and calm interaction can be as important as physical activity.
  • Check paws, coat, ears and general condition as appropriate, and keep up with preventative care agreed with your vet.

Training and behaviour

Reward-based training helps dogs learn what works. Break skills into manageable steps, reward the behaviour you want and keep sessions short. Avoid methods based on fear, pain or intimidation. If a dog is frightened, create distance from the trigger and allow recovery rather than forcing an interaction.

Health and changes in behaviour

Sudden changes in behaviour can be linked to pain, illness or stress. Contact your veterinary practice if a change is new, persistent or concerning. For ongoing behaviour difficulties, ask your vet about an appropriate referral to a suitably qualified, welfare-centred behaviour professional.

Prepare for emergencies

Keep your veterinary practice and out-of-hours details accessible. Contact a vet promptly for breathing difficulty, collapse, significant bleeding, suspected poisoning, severe pain or other serious symptoms. Do not rely on a website to diagnose an emergency.
