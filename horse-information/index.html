Key areas to consider

Use these topics as a starting point, and adapt guidance to the species and individual animal.

01 · Guide

Daily care and observation

Monitor appetite, water intake, demeanour, movement and manure. Consistent routines help make changes easier to spot.

02 · Guide

Feeding and body condition

Feeding plans should reflect workload, age, health and individual needs. Make dietary changes gradually and seek veterinary or qualified nutrition advice.

03 · Guide

Movement and environment

Provide suitable turnout, movement, shelter, safe fencing and social contact appropriate to the horse and circumstances.

04 · Guide

Handling and preventative care

Use safe handling practices and maintain a plan for farriery, dental care, vaccination and parasite control with appropriate professionals.

In-depth guide

A routine for horse and pony welfare

Good horse care relies on daily observation, suitable feeding, movement, social contact, safe handling and preventative care tailored to the individual.

Daily observation

Check appetite, water intake, demeanour, droppings, movement and the condition of legs and feet. Notice changes early and keep a record if a problem is developing.

Feed, movement and environment

Build feeding plans around age, health, workload and body condition, and make changes gradually. Provide suitable movement, turnout, shelter, safe fencing and appropriate social contact. Seek advice from a vet or qualified equine nutrition professional if unsure.

Preventative and emergency care

Agree a plan for dental care, farriery, vaccination and parasite control with relevant professionals. Colic signs, breathing difficulty, serious wounds, inability to bear weight or sudden severe distress require urgent veterinary advice.
