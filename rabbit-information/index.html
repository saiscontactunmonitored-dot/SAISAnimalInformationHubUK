Key areas to consider

Use these topics as a starting point, and adapt guidance to the species and individual animal.

01 · Guide

Diet and digestion

A rabbit’s diet needs to be based around suitable grass or hay, with fresh water and appropriate additional foods. Ask a rabbit-savvy vet about diet changes.

02 · Guide

Space and companionship

Provide room to move, explore, stretch and hide, with secure housing and safe exercise space. Consider compatible companionship and seek specialist advice on introductions.

03 · Guide

Handling and behaviour

Many rabbits find being lifted stressful. Interact at their level, support the body properly when handling is necessary and watch for signs of fear.

04 · Guide

Health and prevention

Discuss vaccination, neutering, dental care and parasite risks with a vet experienced in rabbits. Reduced eating or droppings can be urgent.

In-depth guide

Building a suitable home for rabbits

Rabbits need considerably more than a small hutch. Plan their living space around movement, shelter, companionship, choice and protection from weather and predators.

Space and layout

  • Provide a large, secure living area with a connected exercise space, allowing rabbits to run, hop, stretch out and stand upright without restriction.
  • Include a dry sheltered sleeping area, multiple hiding places, comfortable non-slip flooring and room to separate resting, eating and toileting areas.
  • Check the enclosure regularly for escape routes, sharp edges, damaged materials and gaps a rabbit could become trapped in.

Companionship and handling

Rabbits are social animals and generally benefit from a compatible rabbit companion. Introductions need careful management; ask a rabbit-experienced rescue or veterinary professional for help if you are unsure. Interact at ground level and avoid lifting unless necessary, because being picked up can be frightening and a struggling rabbit can injure itself.

Food, water and daily checks

Offer a suitable high-fibre diet based on grass or hay, fresh drinking water and appropriate additional foods. Make changes gradually and check that every rabbit can access resources. Observe appetite, droppings, movement and behaviour every day.

When to contact a vet

Reduced appetite or droppings, a hunched posture, unusual quietness, difficulty breathing, injury or signs of pain need prompt veterinary advice. Rabbits can deteriorate quickly; do not wait for an online response if you are concerned.

Important information

Rabbits can deteriorate quickly when unwell. Contact a veterinary practice urgently if a rabbit stops eating, produces markedly fewer droppings, appears in pain or becomes unusually quiet.
