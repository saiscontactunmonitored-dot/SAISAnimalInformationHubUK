Reptile and exotic animal information

Reptiles and other exotic species need carefully controlled, species-specific environments and specialist veterinary support.

Key areas to consider

Use these topics as a starting point, and adapt guidance to the species and individual animal.

01 · Guide

Research before acquiring

Confirm adult size, lifespan, diet, environmental requirements, legal responsibilities and access to an experienced vet before bringing an animal home.

02 · Guide

Temperature, light and humidity

Requirements vary considerably by species. Use reliable measurements and species-specific husbandry guidance rather than guesswork.

03 · Guide

Nutrition and hydration

Diet, supplements and water provision depend on the species and life stage. Seek specialist guidance before making significant changes.

04 · Guide

Safe handling and hygiene

Minimise unnecessary handling, use appropriate hygiene measures and consider risks to both the animal and people in the household.

In-depth guide

Planning care for reptiles and exotic animals

Reptiles and exotic pets have specialised needs. Before acquiring one, check the species’ adult size, lifespan, diet, environmental requirements, legal responsibilities and access to an experienced veterinary practice.

Measure the environment

Temperature, humidity, light and UVB requirements vary by species. Use suitable measuring equipment and follow reputable species-specific guidance; do not rely on touch or guesswork to assess conditions.

Feeding and hygiene

Diet, supplements and hydration vary widely. Use reliable specialist guidance and avoid sudden changes without advice. Wash hands after handling animals or cleaning equipment and keep their equipment separate from food preparation areas.

Veterinary care and welfare

Find a veterinary practice with relevant exotic-animal experience before a problem occurs. Changes in appetite, shedding, movement, breathing or behaviour can indicate a problem. Never release an unwanted non-native animal into the wild.
