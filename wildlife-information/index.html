Key areas to consider

Use these topics as a starting point, and adapt guidance to the species and individual animal.

01 · Guide

Observe before intervening

A young animal alone is not always abandoned. Keep your distance and seek reliable species-specific advice before moving wildlife.

02 · Guide

Reduce common hazards

Keep pets under control where appropriate, avoid disturbing nests or roosts and make gardens safer through thoughtful planting and waste management.

03 · Guide

If wildlife appears injured

Avoid unnecessary handling. Contact a recognised wildlife rescue or veterinary practice for advice and follow their instructions.

04 · Guide

Protect habitats

Provide suitable native planting, water and shelter where appropriate, and avoid pesticides or actions that disturb wildlife.

In-depth guide

Responding responsibly to wildlife

The safest first step is often to observe from a distance. Young wildlife seen alone is not always abandoned, and unnecessary handling can cause stress or separate animals from their parents.

If you find an animal

Keep people and pets at a safe distance. Do not feed, medicate or move wildlife unless a recognised rescue or veterinary professional advises you to do so. Describe what you can see and where the animal is when asking for help.

Make gardens safer

Reduce hazards by securing rubbish, avoiding harmful chemicals where possible, providing suitable native planting and checking for wildlife before cutting hedges or disturbing potential nesting sites. Keep pets under control where wildlife may be vulnerable.

Find appropriate help

Contact a recognised wildlife rescue or veterinary practice for an injured animal and follow their instructions. Some species are legally protected, and some situations require official advice. Do not attempt to raise wildlife yourself without specialist guidance.
